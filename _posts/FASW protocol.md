#Filtered Artificial Sea Water (FASW) Protocol
##*Method from the Master’s thesis of: Ben Berman*


Filtered artificial seawater (FASW) is a controlled experimental medium ideal for laboratory usage as it mimics natural seawater in salinity and ionic composotion while minimizing the presence of external contaminents and unwanted microorganisims. 

###Methods:
- Obtain a large (1 L - 2 L) Erlenmeyer flask, and place it on a stir plate with a stir bar gently placed at the bottom of the flask.
- Measure 1 L of double deionized H<sub>2</sub>O (ddH<sub>2</sub>O), and add the majority (750 mL - 800 mL) to the flask. Set the stiring at a gentle pace to swirl the water.
- The Red Sea Salt ([Red Sea Ltd.](https://g1.redseafish.com/red-sea-salts/red-sea-salt/)) artificial sea salt mixture was used for this experiment. Small amounts were added to the swirling ddH<sub>2</sub>O until a final salinity between 38-40 was reached 
	- This is equivalent to how artifical sea water is made for the aquariums used Leon H. Charney School of Marine Sciences in Haifa, Israel.
- Remove the stir bar from the flask. In a sterilized chemical hood, pour the 1 L solution through a sterilized 0.22 uL filter into a sterilized 1 L media storage bottle.
- Seal the bottle tightly in the chemical hood, and place in storage until needed.
